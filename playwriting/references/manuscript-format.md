# Manuscript format

If the user supplied a script, match theirs exactly, including habits that differ from the list below (`build_play.py --describe` shows them). Writers notice when it changes. For a new script:

- US Letter, 12 pt readable serif or Courier, 1.5" left margin if it will be bound, 1" elsewhere, numbered pages.
- Title page; then characters, setting, time.
- Character cue in capitals, centred or on a steady indent, kept with its first line.
- Dialogue from the left margin.
- Directions in parentheses on one consistent indent, on their own line, including mid-speech. Italics optional.
- "(continued)" after the cue when a speech crosses a page.
- White space is a courtesy to readers.
- Contests and theatres set their own rules; those win.

## The page before the play

Characters, setting and time are the first writing anyone reads. Match how plays do it, in the writer's own layout.

- **Characters.** Age, then two or three traits that make them want things ("forties, proud of her driving"). Traits you can watch, not a summary of what they learn or where they end up. Leave the theme to the play. Two people's descriptions can set each other off.
- **Setting.** Where we are, in a sentence or two, as you'd tell a friend. Name only what matters. No measurements, staging advice or notes about limits: the director will see what it needs.
- **Time.** One sentence that carries the shape of the play ("A single night, and the morning after"). Don't list it scene by scene; each scene's first direction says when we are. If you move that out of the time page, make sure Scene One says it too.
- Rewrite all three once the scenes are settled.

Rough timing: about a minute a page in standard single-spaced dialogue format. Double spacing breaks that estimate, so time a read instead.
